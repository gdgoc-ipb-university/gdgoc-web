# Dashboard: roles, assignments, and members

`/dashboard` is the signed-in area for members and administrators, the home of Apresiasi, and the full Bogor Run leaderboard. It uses Google sign-in (Better Auth on Convex) and the onboarding member profile. No extra OAuth scopes or environment variables are required.

## Layout and icons

Signed-in pages use a sticky sidebar grouped into Belajar, Kelola (staff only), and Akun, with the account, role badges, a link back to the site, and sign-out at the bottom. The brand and the account block stay in view and only the nav scrolls (the sidebar carries `data-lenis-prevent` so the wheel reaches it); windows under 760px tall get a tighter sidebar, and under 480px the whole sidebar scrolls. Below 960px it becomes a drawer opened from the top bar; opening moves focus into it, and Escape or the backdrop closes it. Sign-in, onboarding, role prompt, and deactivated states keep the public site header instead.

Icons are [Pixelarticons](https://pixelarticons.com) (MIT) path data copied into `src/components/pixel-icons.tsx`; add new icons there as 24×24 paths. Every choice list uses `Select` (`src/components/select.tsx`, React Aria): a field-height trigger with a pixel chevron and a listbox popover, with label, hint, error, and an inline variant (rows per page); it submits through a hidden native select when given a `name`. Fields, selects, the search box and tool buttons share `--control-h` (46px); compact controls use `--control-h-sm` (38px). Staff tables build on `src/components/dashboard/data-table.tsx`: square icon buttons whose accessible name is also their tooltip, sortable column headers, numbered pagination, and confirm dialogs (React Aria `Menu` and `Modal`). The `gamepad` icon for Papan skor and the `id-card` icon for Kartu anggota are hand-drawn in the same grid, not copied from Pixelarticons.

## Roles

- **Owner:** a verified Google email in the Convex `APPRECIATION_ADMIN_EMAILS` allowlist. Owners are permanent: they cannot be demoted or deactivated from the dashboard, and they always review Apresiasi. Only owners can promote or demote admins and grant or revoke Apresiasi review.
- **Admin:** a member promoted by an owner, stored as `memberProfiles.role = "admin"`. Admins manage assignments, see all submissions, review Apresiasi, and can deactivate or reactivate members (not admins or owners). Demoting an admin to member ends the review access that came with the role.
- **Assignment reviewer:** an active member staff added to one assignment's `reviewers` (see *Assignment reviewers*). Not a role: it applies to that assignment only.
- **Door helper:** an active member staff added to one gathering's `helpers` (see [Gatherings](#gatherings)). Not a role either: they check people in at that gathering only.
- **Cohort mentor:** an active member staff made a mentor of one role in a cohort (see [Cohorts](#cohorts)). Not a role either: they review that role's assignments in that cohort only, and see that role's participants by name.
- **Member:** any verified account that has completed onboarding. There is no approval step. A member with an active, claimed enrolment in a cohort is also that cohort's **participant**.

**Apresiasi reviewer** is a permission, not a role: `memberProfiles.appreciationReviewer`, granted and revoked by owners from the members page for active members, typically the Media & Creative people who prepare the posts. Owners and admins review by role, so the grant is not offered for them. Active Core Team and BoD members tagged **Media & Creative** review too, once staff have set or confirmed that tag (`memberProfiles.tagConfirmedAt`; `reviewsByTag` in `convex/auth.ts`). Self-declared tags never count: a member who picks the tag in onboarding shows "belum dikonfirmasi" on the members page until staff choose **Konfirmasi peran komunitas** (or save the tag from "Ubah peran komunitas"), and a member who later changes their own tag loses the confirmation, even if they change it back. Both events go to the access log. `isReviewer` in `convex/auth.ts` is the single check (owner, an active admin, a granted reviewer, or a confirmed Media & Creative tag, never while deactivated), used by `appreciations.queue`/`search`/`history`/`review`, `auth.viewer`, and `dashboard.viewer`. Deactivation suspends the permission without clearing it; revoking removes the field. A reviewer who is not staff sees only "Tinjau apresiasi" under Kelola.

**Tech Support handler** is a permission like it: `memberProfiles.techSupportHandler`, granted and revoked by owners from the members page ("Jadikan pengelola Tech Support") for active members who are not staff, typically Community & External. Owners and active admins handle the queue by role. `handlesTechSupport` in `convex/access.ts` is the single check (owner, an active admin, or an active granted member), used by `techSupport.queue` and `handle` and returned as `techSupportHandler` by `dashboard.viewer` for the sidebar. Grants and revocations go to the access log. Who can *submit* a request is `requireTechSupportRequester` in the same file: any active, onboarded member, with no community tag required.

These access roles are separate from the **community tag**. Everyone registers as **Member** during onboarding; only owners and admins assign **Core Team** (with a division) and **BoD** (Board of Directors, with an optional division) from the members page ([ADR 0007](decisions/0007-staff-assign-community-tags.md), #156). The profile shows the tag read-only, with whether staff confirmed it, and no member mutation accepts a tag. Core Team tags members chose themselves before #156 stay, unconfirmed, until staff change or confirm them. Community tags grant no permissions; they only decide which assignments a member is given (see [Audience](#audience)). New Apresiasi drafts prefill the tag (Member, Core Team, or BoD), and the server accepts "BoD" as a submission's role only from a member tagged BoD.

Deactivation sets `memberProfiles.deactivatedAt`. A deactivated account can still sign in but sees only a notice; every assignment query and mutation rejects it. Its submissions are kept. It also disappears from every Bogor Run board, and reactivation brings it back unless staff hid the player separately (see [Moderation](#names-and-moderation)). An admin must be demoted before deactivation, and a deactivated member must be reactivated before promotion. Nobody can change their own status. `accessUpdatedBy` records the last account that changed a role or status, and the access log (`/dashboard/anggota/riwayat`) keeps every change.

All rules are enforced in Convex (`convex/access.ts`, `convex/dashboard.ts`, `convex/assignments.ts`, `convex/bogorRun.ts`); the UI only hides actions the server would refuse.

### Decision: owners stay in configuration

Owners come from the `APPRECIATION_ADMIN_EMAILS` environment variable on the Convex deployment, not from the database, and the dashboard cannot add, remove or demote them. Decided for E7 (#35), 9 October 2026.

- **Why:** the chapter has one or two owners a year, and they change at the handover. A database owner role would need its own guard against the last owner removing themselves, and a stolen admin session could not promote itself to owner. The allowlist needs neither.
- **Cost:** changing owners needs someone with Convex dashboard or CLI access to edit the variable. The handover checklist should include it.
- **How to change:** set `APPRECIATION_ADMIN_EMAILS` on the production deployment to the comma-separated Google emails (see `docs/APRESIASI.md`). The change applies on the next request; no deploy is needed. An owner who is removed becomes a member, or an admin if `memberProfiles.role` is still set.

## Assignments

An assignment has a title, a slug, plain-text instructions (line breaks preserved), an audience, a deadline, a maximum score (default 100), and a status:

- `draft`: visible only to owners and admins. It can be deleted while it has no submissions.
- `published`: visible to active members in its audience, who can submit and resubmit.
- `closed`: visible read-only. No new or updated submissions.

### Audience

Each assignment is given to one or more community tags, ticked on the create and edit forms under "Untuk siapa": **Member**, **Core Team**, and **BoD** (#79). Ticking all three gives it to every member. BoD is its own group: a Core Team assignment does not reach BoD unless BoD is ticked too. At least one tag is required, and new assignments start with Member only. Once a cohort exists, "Untuk siapa" also offers **Peserta program**, which targets a cohort's roles instead (see [Cohorts](#cohorts)).

`assignments.audience` stores the tags. Assignments created before the field existed have none and are read as Core Team only (`assignmentAudience`, `legacyAudience` in `src/lib/assignment.ts`); every assignment in production on 9 October 2026 was for the core team. Editing one from a tab loaded before this change keeps its audience instead of applying the default.

A member outside the audience gets nothing: the assignment is missing from their list and overview, its link shows "Tugas tidak ditemukan", and `generateUploadUrl`, `attachFile`, and `submit` refuse it (`reaches` in `convex/assignments.ts`). Owners and admins are never in an audience, but they see and manage every assignment. An assignment's reviewers keep the reviewer view whatever its audience. Staff can change the audience at any time. A member dropped from it keeps their submission and review, which staff still see and score, but the assignment no longer appears for them, an open revision request included.

Staff see the audience as an "Untuk …" badge on the assignment list and page.

### Slugs

The slug fills in from the title as it is typed (lowercase ASCII words joined by hyphens, `&` becomes `dan`, accents removed, at most 60 characters) until an admin edits it; "Samakan dengan judul" re-links it. `assignments.slugPreview` shows the final slug live. A slug used by another assignment gets `-2`, `-3`, and so on; `baru` is reserved for the create page. Every slug an assignment has had stays in `assignmentSlugs`, so renamed links, and older ID-based links, redirect to the current URL. "Salin link" copies the full slug URL.

An assignment with submissions cannot return to draft or be deleted; close it instead. Deadlines are entered and shown in WIB (UTC+7). Edits use revision checks, so a stale tab cannot silently overwrite a newer version.

### Submissions and files

A submission is a rich-text answer (up to 5,000 characters) and/or up to five files of at most 10 MB each: PDF, PNG, JPG, WebP, TXT, ZIP, DOCX, PPTX, or XLSX. Each member has one submission per assignment. Resubmitting replaces the answer and file set and updates the submission time.

### Rich-text answers

The answer uses a [Tiptap](https://tiptap.dev) editor (`src/components/dashboard/rich-text-editor.tsx`). Its toolbar follows the WAI-ARIA toolbar pattern: one tab stop, with arrow keys, Home, and End to move between tools. It offers bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, inline code, two heading levels, bulleted and numbered lists, quotes, code blocks, links (Ctrl/⌘ + K opens an inline link bar), undo, and redo. Standard shortcuts apply, and a counter shows the 5,000-character limit. Toolbar icons come from `src/components/pixel-icons.tsx`. Pixelarticons has no text-formatting icons, so bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, and numbered list were drawn in the same 24×24 pixel grid.

Answers are stored twice: `answerDoc`, the editor's JSON, and `answer`, a plain-text copy used for previews and older clients. `src/lib/rich-text.ts` rebuilds every document through an allowlist, on the server in `assignments.submit` and again before rendering:

- Only known nodes and marks are accepted. Any other node or mark rejects the whole document.
- Links must be absolute `http:`, `https:`, or `mailto:` URLs; any other link is removed and its text kept.
- Depth, node count, and JSON size are bounded.
- The character limit is counted the way the editor counts: text plus one per line break or divider.

`RichTextView` renders the stored JSON with React elements rather than `innerHTML`. Anything that fails the allowlist falls back to the plain text. Earlier plain-text answers open in the editor as paragraphs, and clients from before this change can still send plain text, which clears the stored document.

**Late** is computed as `submittedAt > dueAt` whenever it is read, so moving a deadline also updates the flags. Work after the deadline is accepted and marked late until an admin closes the assignment. Resubmitting after the deadline marks previously on-time work late; the form warns about this.

Upload flow:

Files are added from the dropzone (`src/components/dashboard/file-dropzone.tsx`) by drag and drop or the "Pilih file" button. The zone highlights while a file is dragged anywhere over the page. A file dropped outside the zone is ignored, rather than opening in the browser and leaving the form. Extra files beyond the five slots are skipped with a message.

Images are optimised in the browser first (`src/lib/image-optimize.ts`). PNG, JPEG, and WebP files are decoded (honouring EXIF orientation), downscaled to at most 2560 px on the long edge, and re-encoded as WebP at quality 0.82. The WebP is used only when it is smaller; browsers that cannot encode WebP keep the original. Re-encoding drops EXIF metadata such as GPS location. Because compression happens first, images up to 25 MB are accepted if the result fits the 10 MB limit. The file list shows the saving. PDF, Office, and ZIP files are already compressed containers, so they are uploaded unchanged; TXT files are small. The server still validates the final name, type, and size.

1. `assignments.generateUploadUrl` returns a Convex storage upload URL when the assignment is open.
2. The browser posts the file with its type as `Content-Type`, using `XMLHttpRequest` so each file shows a progress bar and can be cancelled.
3. `assignments.attachFile` checks the stored size and type against the extension, and records a pending `submissionFiles` row owned by the member. A rejected file is deleted, and a message is returned instead of an error, so the deletion commits. An upload ID can be claimed only once.
4. `assignments.submit` attaches the selected pending files, and deletes previously attached files that were removed.

The hourly `remove unclaimed assignment uploads` cron (`convex/crons.ts`) deletes pending files older than 24 hours, plus storage objects created 24–72 hours ago that no `submissionFiles` row references (uploads that were never registered). **Assignment submissions are currently the only feature that stores files.** A new storage feature must be added to that check, or its files will be removed.

File URLs come from `ctx.storage.getUrl`. They are unguessable, but not authenticated, and are returned only to the submitting member and to admins.

### Who has not submitted

Below the submissions, a published or closed assignment lists the active, onboarded members without a submission (`assignments.missing`), alphabetically, with a "Salin daftar nama dan email" button that copies one `Nama — email` line per member for a reminder. Only members in the assignment's audience are counted ("x dari y member sasaran"). Owners and admins are not expected to submit and are left out of both the list and the count; deactivated accounts are hidden.

### Scores and feedback

Staff review each submission from the assignment page: a whole-number score from 0 to the assignment's `maxScore` (`assignmentMaxScore`, 100 for assignments created before the field existed), feedback of up to 2,000 characters, or both. `assignments.review` stores the latest review on the submission (`score`, `feedback`, `reviewedAt`, `reviewedBy`) and appends a row to `submissionReviews`, so re-reviews keep their history. Saving feedback without a score clears a previous score; the form prefills the current values so that is a deliberate act.

The review carries the submission `revision` the reviewer saw. If the member resubmitted in between, the server refuses with "Member memperbarui kirimannya…" and the reviewer reloads. A resubmission after a review keeps the score but is **stale** (`isStaleReview`: `submittedAt > reviewedAt`): staff see "Diperbarui setelah dinilai" and a prompt to look again, the member sees that the score applies to the earlier version, and `reviewedCount` on the staff list counts only current reviews.

Members see a result card above their form (or above the closed notice): the score out of the maximum, the feedback, when and by whom (the reviewer's display name, never their email), plus "Dinilai x/y" badges on the assignment list. Only the owner of a submission and staff can read a review.

### Rubrics

An assignment can carry a **rubric**: up to 8 criteria, each with a name (≤ 80 characters) and a whole-number maximum of at least 1, with the maxes summing to at most 1,000. With a rubric, `maxScore` is the sum of the maxes (the form shows it read-only), and members see the criteria and their points under the instructions. Blank rows in the editor are dropped on save.

Reviewing a rubric assignment means filling one point value per criterion, in rubric order; `assignments.review` takes them as `points`, checks each against its criterion's max, and stores the sum as `score` together with a `breakdown` (each criterion's name, max and points, as the rubric stood at that moment) on the submission and on the `submissionReviews` row. Sending a bare `score` for a rubric assignment is refused, as are a `points` array whose length does not match the current rubric and `points` for an assignment whose rubric was removed (the reviewer reloads). Feedback alone clears both score and breakdown. The member's result card shows the breakdown as it was scored, so a later rubric edit does not relabel a past review; the review form prefills earlier points only when they were given against the current criteria. Catalyst's capstone rubric (the product plus each role's contribution) is one such rubric, written by Program & Development.

### Assignment reviewers

Staff can add members as reviewers of one assignment from its page ("Penilai tugas ini"), e.g. a Catalyst role's mentors, up to 10 per assignment. `assignments.setReviewer` accepts only active, onboarded members; admins and owners already review every assignment. A reviewer of an assignment gets its staff view: the submissions with answers and files, scoring (rubric points included), revision requests, the "who hasn't submitted" list, and the CSV export. Emails are hidden from reviewers in all of them, and they do not see the edit, publish, close, delete or reviewer controls. Their assignments page lists "Tugas yang kamu nilai" with how many submissions wait for a review (`assignments.reviewing`). Every review path (`submissions`, `missing`, `exportPage`, `review`, `requestRevision`, `cancelRevision`) runs the same `requireReviewer` check, and an assignment's reviewers are left out of its "who hasn't submitted" counts. Removing a reviewer, or deactivating the account, ends the access.

### Revision requests

From the review form, "Minta revisi" asks one member to revise their submission. The note is the text in the feedback field, and it is required. `assignments.requestRevision` takes the submission revision the reviewer saw (a resubmission in between is refused, as with scoring), stores the note as the submission's feedback, sets `revisionRequestedAt`, and keeps the score until the revision is reviewed. Every request is also a `submissionReviews` row with `revisionRequested: true`.

While a request is open, that member can upload and resubmit even after the assignment is closed; everyone else still sees it closed. The member sees "Peninjau meminta revisi." with the note, a "Revisi diminta" badge on the assignment, in the list and on the overview, and the form reopens. Resubmitting clears the request, and the review then shows as *Diperbarui setelah dinilai* until it is reviewed again. Staff see "Revisi diminta" on the submission, a count in the list header, and can withdraw an unanswered request with "Batalkan permintaan revisi" (`assignments.cancelRevision`). Submissions with an open request are not counted as reviewed.

### Export

"Unduh CSV" next to the submission counts downloads every submission of the assignment with its review: name, campus, submitted at (WIB), late, review status (*Belum dinilai*, *Dinilai*, *Diperbarui setelah dinilai*), score, maximum, feedback, reviewer, reviewed at (WIB) and attachment names, sorted by name. With a rubric, each current criterion gets a points column, filled only when the review was scored against the same criteria, and *Rincian rubrik* holds the breakdown as it was scored. The email column is included for owners only, because the file leaves the dashboard; admins get the same file without it.

The file is built in the browser from `assignments.exportPage`, read 100 submissions at a time, as UTF-8 with a byte order mark and CRLF line ends so Excel opens it cleanly. Text cells that start with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return get a leading apostrophe so a spreadsheet does not run them as formulas. The builder is `src/lib/assignment-export.ts`.

## Cohorts

A **cohort** is one run of a program with its own roles, such as GDGoC Catalyst 2026/27 with Hacker, Hipster and Hustler (E4, #32; this part is #103). It holds who takes part and in which role and team, who mentors each role, and which assignments are for it. Staff see and edit participants and mentors on `/dashboard/program/<slug>` (#104, #105) and target tasks at a cohort's roles in the assignment editor (#105). Cohorts themselves are created with `npx convex run cohorts:seedCatalyst` (idempotent) or by staff through `cohorts.create`; there is no form for that yet.

- **Cohorts and roles.** `cohorts.create` and `update` (staff) take a name, a slug, one to eight roles (a lowercase key and a display name) and an optional attendance target, and `update` also sets the status (`setup`, `active`, `ended`). A role still used by an enrolment, a mentor or an assignment cannot be removed; its name can change. `src/lib/cohort.ts` holds the validation and the Catalyst defaults.
- **Enrolments.** One row per participant, keyed by email (trimmed, lowercased), because accepted applicants are imported before most of them have an account. When an account with that verified Google email loads the dashboard, `cohorts.claimMine` sets the row's `ownerId`; it is idempotent, never moves a row that is already claimed, and does nothing for an unverified email. Every check reads by `ownerId` after that. A `withdrawn` enrolment keeps its row and submissions but reaches nothing.
- **Cohort assignments.** The editor's "Untuk siapa" picks either community tags or **Peserta program**: a cohort and one, several or all of its roles (`cohortId` and `cohortRoles` on `assignments.create` and `update`, checked against the cohort; choosing every role is stored as no roles, which means all of them, including a role added later; an update that sends no `cohortId` keeps the current target, and `null` moves the task back to community tags). Such an assignment goes to that cohort's active, claimed participants whose role is in `cohortRoles`, and its community-tag audience is ignored. Staff see it as always; members outside it get "Tugas tidak ditemukan" like any assignment outside their audience (`reaches` in `convex/assignments.ts`). Badges read "Untuk GDGoC Catalyst 2026/27 · Hacker". Participants are community members too (#32, decision 4, as proposed): they onboard as Member and also get Member-audience tasks, so a community task not meant for them goes to Core Team or BoD. Due dates are set by hand until the session dates are known (decision 9).
- **Who has not submitted, for a cohort task.** `assignments.missing` counts from the roster, not from member profiles: every active participant of the task's roles, minus its reviewers and deactivated accounts. A participant who has not signed in yet shows as "Belum masuk"; staff see the enrolment email of each one, and mentors and reviewers see names only.
- **Import.** **Impor peserta** takes the accepted list from the oprec as a CSV file (comma, semicolon or tab separated, with or without a byte order mark) or cells pasted from Google Sheets, header row included, so it works with whatever the results sheet looks like (#32, decision 3). It guesses the email, name and role columns from the headers and maps each distinct role answer (e.g. "Hacker (Software Engineer)") to the one role it names; staff can change both, and an answer mapped to "Jangan impor" is skipped. Before anything is written it shows how many rows are new, updated, unchanged and skipped, with each skipped line and why: an invalid email, an email seen earlier in the file, an empty name, or an unmapped role (`planImport` in `src/lib/enrolment-import.ts`). Only new and changed rows are sent, 200 per `cohorts.importEnrolments` call, which checks them again. Rows match by email: a new email is added, and linked at once when an account with that verified email already exists; a known one gets the file's name and role, and a role change is logged. An import never changes a withdrawal. Up to 1,000 rows per import.
- **Roster changes.** `cohorts.setRole`, `setStatus` (withdrawn and back; the row and its submissions stay) and `setEmail` (for a participant who signs in with another Gmail than the one on the form: the row leaves its previous account and links to the new email's account if there is one) each go to the access log as `enrolmentRole`, `enrolmentStatus` or `enrolmentEmail`, with the cohort and enrolment on the row. A participant who has not signed in has no account ID yet, so the log names them by their oprec name while the enrolment exists.
- **Bevy export.** Owners get **CSV untuk Bevy** on the roster: the active participants' email and name in the template of Bevy's "Import members" on gdg.community.dev (`email,first_name,last_name`, UTF-8 with a byte order mark and CRLF, like the other exports), so they can be added as chapter members. The first word of a name is the first name and the rest the last name; a one-word name has no last name. The profile name is used once a participant has signed in. `cohorts.exportBevy` is owners only, like the members CSV, because the file is personal data leaving the site. Bevy asks the importer to confirm the members consented and how: the dialog says to tick it only if the oprec form asked, and offers a sentence to paste (`src/lib/bevy-export.ts`). `/privasi` says names and emails may be registered there with consent.
- **Onboarding.** `cohorts.myInvitation` tells a signed-in account with an active enrolment for its email which program and role they were accepted into. Onboarding then greets them as a participant on the first screen and asks for "Sekolah atau kampus" and "Jurusan atau program studi", since SMK students have no campus or study programme.
- **Mentors.** A `cohortMentors` row makes a member a mentor of one role. Staff add and remove them under **Mentor per peran** on the cohort page with `cohorts.setMentor`, by account like `assignments.setReviewer`: an active, onboarded member who is not staff and not an active participant of the same cohort, up to 10 per role. Each change goes to the access log as `cohortMentor` (`off` → role key, role key → `off`). Mentors review every assignment of that cohort that includes their role, with the same view and limits as an assignment reviewer (no emails, no management): submissions, scoring, revision requests, the missing list and the export. Those assignments appear under "Tugas yang kamu nilai" (`canReview` in `convex/assignments.ts`) without adding the mentor to each. A mentor of Hacker cannot open, list or score a Hipster-only task. `cohorts.mentoring` shows mentors their roles' active participants by name only, under "Peserta yang kamu dampingi" (#32, decision 5, as proposed; attendance is #109). `assignments.reviewers` still adds one-off reviewers.
- **Teams.** Teams are formed after session 1 (#107). A `teams` row (`cohortId`, `name`) groups participants of one cohort, and membership is `enrolments.teamId`, so a participant is in at most one team per cohort. Staff use **Tim** on the cohort page: `cohorts.createTeam` and `renameTeam` take a name of up to 60 characters that no other team of the cohort uses, ignoring case; `setTeam` puts an active participant (signed in or not) in a team of the same cohort, moving them out of any other, or takes them out with `null`; `deleteTeam` clears `teamId` on its enrolments and removes the team. A withdrawn participant cannot be put in a team; withdrawing keeps their `teamId`, but they leave every team view until restored. The section shows each team's members and role mix ("Hacker 2 · Hipster 1"), and the active participants without a team; the roster has a Tim column. `cohorts.myTeams` gives a participant each team they are in with its name and the other active members' names and roles, never emails, shown as "Tim kamu" on their Tugas page. Team changes grant no access, so they are not in the access log.
- **Participant card.** The overview (`/dashboard`) shows each participant a card per active enrolment in a cohort that has not ended (#106): the cohort, their role with its icon from `/program/catalyst` (`catalyst.roles` in `src/lib/catalyst.ts`, matched by role key), their team and teammates by name and role, or "Tim belum dibentuk." until staff put them in one, and the published tasks of that cohort for their role, soonest due first, each linking to the task with its due date and "Terkumpul", "Belum dikumpulkan" or "Revisi diminta". `cohorts.mine` returns only the caller's own enrolment, team name, teammates' names and roles, and their own submission status: no emails, no other participants' rows, no other roles' tasks, drafts or closed tasks. Members who are not enrolled, withdrawn participants and ended cohorts get no card. There is no next-session line and nothing in its place: the session dates (#32, decision 9) are not known yet, so it comes with sessions in #109.
- **Deletion.** Deleting an account also deletes its claimed enrolments, any unclaimed enrolment for its email, and its mentor rows, counted as `enrolments` and `mentorGrants` in `accountDeletions`.

What happens to a cohort's data after the program ends is open (#32, decision 8); `/privasi` says what is held and who sees it, not yet how long.

## Member cards

Every active, onboarded member has one member card, like a student ID, for checking in at the chapter's own gatherings (E10, #113; this part is #114). Public events stay on Bevy (decision 1).

- **Number.** `cards.ensure` issues the card the first time the member opens it: the chapter year's next number, such as `2627-0042` for the 42nd card of 2026/27 (`cardPrefix` and `cardNumber` in `src/lib/member-card.ts`), and a token of 128 random bits. A `cardCounters` row per chapter year counts the numbers given out, in the same transaction, so two members opening their cards at once never share a number, and a deleted account's number is never given out again. Opening the card in a later chapter year gives it that year's next number and a new token. The chapter year is `chapterPeriod` in `src/lib/chapter.ts`, shared with Apresiasi: update it at the July handover, or every card reads as expired after 1 July.
- **Status.** A card is valid while its holder is onboarded and active, staff have not blocked it, and it was opened in the current chapter year (`cardStatus` in `convex/cards.ts`). Otherwise it is `revoked`, `inactive` (a deactivated holder, read from `deactivatedAt`, so deactivation needs no extra write), `expired`, or `unknown` (no card has that token).
- **Scanning.** `cards.verify` answers anyone. A guest, or a member who is not the holder, gets the name, community tag (and whether staff confirmed it) and chapter year of a valid card (decision 5), and the same `invalid` for every other status, so a token never confirms that an account exists. The same view serves as proof of membership outside the chapter's gatherings, for example at a partner venue (decision 6). Active staff and the holder get the whole card with the exact status: number, the photo (the member's uploaded one, else the Google one, see [Profile photos](#profile-photos)), campus, study program and tag. No view includes the email.
- **Replacing the QR.** `cards.reissue` gives the holder's card a new token. The old QR stops working at once, including saved images and screenshots of it. A blocked card cannot be reissued.
- **Blocking.** `cards.setRevoked` lets staff block a card or lift the block, along the member hierarchy: admins act on members, owners on admins too, and nobody on an owner's card or on their own. Each change goes to the access log as `card`. Staff find **Blokir kartu** and **Buka blokir kartu** under Status in a member's "Kelola" menu once the member has opened their card, and the member's page shows the card number with "Diblokir" while it is blocked.
- **Bulk printing** (#159). Staff print cards for many members at once. `cards.printSelection` returns, page by page, everyone matching the members table's search and filters or a picked list of accounts: name, tag, this chapter year's number or none (no card, or one from an earlier year), blocked, active, and the photo source (`uploaded`, `google` or `initial`), never the token. `cards.issueFor` issues this year's card to up to 50 members at once, exactly as `ensure` does (the counter in the same transaction, a new token), in the order given; a member who already has this year's card is left as it is, so a repeated call issues nothing, and blocked, inactive and not onboarded accounts are skipped and reported. Each card it issues goes to the access log as `cardIssued`, from the old number or `none` to the new one. `cards.printCards` gives the whole card with its token for up to 50 members with this year's card, read when the PDF is drawn.
- **The print file** (#159) is drawn in the browser by `src/components/dashboard/card-pdf.ts`, a dynamic import of the print page, so no other page loads it. It is laid out in `src/lib/card-sheet.ts` and written by `src/lib/pdf.ts`; see [Bulk printing](#bulk-printing).
- **Deletion.** Deleting an account deletes its card, counted as `cards` in `accountDeletions`.
- **The card page** (`/dashboard/kartu`, "Kartu anggota" in the sidebar's Akun group, #115) calls `cards.ensure` on load and shows both sides of the card, each labelled "Bagian depan" or "Bagian belakang": side by side where two fit (up to 340 px each), front then back on a phone. The front has the member's photo (the uploaded one, else the Google one, and the pixel initial when there is none or it fails to load; see [Profile photos](#profile-photos)), name, member number and community tag; the back has the QR, the number, campus and study program, and "Berlaku" with the chapter period. The facts are real text; the QR has a text label.
- **The card design** (#142) is a two-sided CR80 badge, 54 × 85.6 mm portrait, as on the "ID Card" page of the GDGOC 26/27 Figma file, which is the source of truth (the maintainer's edits of 2 November included):
  - Every measure is that page's trim pixel at 300 dpi (638 × 1011) scaled by the side's width / 638 through container query units (`--u` in `src/components/member-card.css`), so a side keeps the printed proportions at any width. Each piece of content sits at its own position from the Figma page (`faceLayout` in `src/components/member-card-face.tsx`).
  - Both sides: the Figma background (pixel fades at the top and bottom and a 4% grid on the paper, `public/cards/card-background.webp`, a lossless crop of the Figma render to the trim), an empty punch slot (13 × 3 mm, a faint rounded outline on the web), clouds and Google-colour confetti, the brackets mark, "GDGoC IPB University" and the chapter year, and art along the bottom.
  - Front: the photo or pixel initial with a hard ink shadow, the name in up to two lines and the number, in one group as Figma's "ID" (12 px apart, so a two-line name pushes the number down). The name wraps word by word, as Figma wraps it, within the clear middle between Tugu Kujang and Gedung AHN: 440 px centred in the 568 px column (99 to 539 px across the trim), so a long name never runs into the landmarks. It sets at 54 px on up to two lines and steps down a pixel at a time to 40 px; a name still too long takes whole words across the full column at 40 px, a single word longer than that breaks anywhere (within the clear area when two lines of it fit), and only a name past two full lines at 40 px (over 46 characters) goes smaller, to 30 px (`nameFit` and `nameArea` in `src/lib/member-card.ts`). The web face and the saved image draw the same lines, so the browser never wraps the name differently. Near the bottom, the role bar in the division's colour, with a hard shadow, holds the community tag only; the card does not mark a confirmed tag. Behind it, Tugu Kujang and Gedung AHN stand oversized on grass and road, cut by the card's edges, with three trees.
  - Back: the QR on a 322 px white plate (3 px border, 8 px padding), 300 px with its quiet zone, which is 25.4 mm printed; then the number, and the campus and validity rows with Pixelarticons icons. Along the bottom, below the QR, the "Tiket masuk program" printer (a ticket printing, `public/cards/tiket-masuk-program.svg`, Figma's frame removed and run through svgo) stands centred on its own shadow over the bottom fade, with no grass or road. It is `BackIllustration` with its layout in `townLayout.back` (`src/components/member-card-face.tsx`), which the door view and the saved image use too, so a new illustration changes in one place.
  - The name, number and stamp are VCR OSD Mono in outlined capitals at Figma's automatic line height; the chapter year is Pixelify Sans Bold; "GDGoC IPB University" is Poppins Medium and the role Space Grotesk Bold; campus Poppins, labels and validity JetBrains Mono.
  - The art is in `public/cards/`. The landmarks and the brackets mark come from the Design System page of the Figma file; the cloud and tree are drawn to match. Their places on each side are `townLayout`.
  - `divisionAccents` in `src/lib/member-card.ts` gives each division one Google colour, darkened until white text passes AA. Secretary Treasurer, BoD without a division and members take the ink.
  - The faces (`MemberCardFront`, `MemberCardBack` and `MemberCard` for the labelled pair) show only the fields they are given, so the scan page can hand the front a guest's limited card.
- **The QR** encodes `<NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL>/k/<token>` and is drawn as SVG on the page by [uqr](https://github.com/unjs/uqr), never by an outside QR service, because the token is the card. Medium error correction keeps it at version 4 (33 modules) with a four-module quiet zone, sparse enough for a phone screen at about 30 cm in a dim room (`src/lib/qr.ts`).
- **At the door.** **Tampilkan QR** shows the back of the card full screen, with the QR on its plate as large as the screen allows, the mark, chapter name and year above it, the number and name under it and the back's landmark strip along the bottom, and keeps the screen awake where the browser allows it. **Simpan kartu** downloads one print-ready PNG with both sides at CR80 trim size, 638 × 1011 px each at 300 dpi, side by side 10 mm apart with crop marks outside the trim and a line saying the image stops working when the QR is replaced (`src/components/dashboard/card-image.ts`). It is drawn on a canvas in the browser from the same background, art and layout as the faces (`townLayout`, so a new back illustration reaches it too), after VCR OSD Mono and the other fonts load, and after the photo is fetched to a blob (no credentials, so the canvas is never tainted by another origin; a photo that does not load falls back to the initial); the punch slot stays empty, as on the printed card. The QR is black on white, 300 px with its quiet zone, 25.4 mm printed as on the Figma page (25 mm is the floor). Each module edge is rounded to a whole pixel, so modules are 7 or 8 px and no edge is anti-aliased. The PNG carries a `pHYs` chunk of 300 dpi (`src/lib/png.ts`), so a print shop or photo app sizes it at 54 × 85.6 mm. The same image works on a phone at a venue with a poor connection; the page says it works until the QR is replaced. **Ganti QR** calls `cards.reissue` after a confirmation that says saved images and screenshots stop working. A blocked card shows "Kartu diblokir" and who to contact, without the QR or these actions.
- **The scan page** (`/k/<token>`, #116) is what a phone camera opens after scanning a card. It is rendered per request, `noindex`, left out of the sitemap, and sends no `Referer`, so the token in its URL does not leak through links; `/k` on its own redirects to the landing. It waits for sign-in to settle before calling `cards.verify`, so staff never see the guest view first. Anyone gets a large verdict, "Kartu anggota aktif" or "Kartu tidak berlaku", with the time of the check in WIB ticking each second, so a screenshot of an old check is easy to tell apart. On a valid card a guest also sees the front of the card with only the name, community tag and chapter year, and a line to match the name with the person; an invalid card shows a guest the front with no holder data. A guest never sees the back. The front carries an "Aktif" or "Tidak berlaku" stamp under the role bar. Staff see both sides, including the photo, the reason a card is not valid, and a link to the member's page; the holder sees both sides of their own card and a link to `/dashboard/kartu`. The back's plate says the QR is not shown there, so the page never repeats the code. Nothing on the page shows the email, and only staff get the link to the member's page.

### Bulk printing

Staff print the chapter's cards as one file instead of collecting each member's saved PNG (#159). The page and the server functions are above; this is the file.

- **Sheet.** A4 portrait with nine slots of 60 × 91.6 mm in three columns and rows, the CR80 trim plus 3 mm of bleed, touching each other: 180 × 274.8 mm, 15 mm from the left and right edges, 8 mm from the top, and 14.2 mm at the bottom for the footer (`grid` in `src/lib/card-sheet.ts`). Each card is one 708 × 1081 px image, the 638 × 1011 px trim and 35 px (2.96 mm) of bleed per side, placed at exactly 300 dpi in the centre of its slot; whole pixels keep the pixel art and the QR modules sharp. The bleed repeats the trim's outermost pixels, so a cut a little off shows the card's own colours.
- **Crop marks** are 0.25 pt black lines, 3 mm long and 1 mm outside the grid's outer bleed edge, at every trim line on all four sides, so none prints on a card. Neighbouring cards share no edge: each gap between two cards is cut twice.
- **Duplex.** Each sheet of fronts is followed by its sheet of backs. On the backs the columns are reversed (`backSlot`: front slot 1, 2, 3 is back slot 3, 2, 1 in each row); because the grid is centred left to right, printing double-sided and flipping on the long edge puts the back of each card behind its front, with the same crop marks on both sides. The PDF asks viewers to print at actual size and double-sided on the long edge (`/PrintScaling /None`, `/Duplex /DuplexFlipLongEdge`), and the page and every footer repeat it. The 3 mm bleed absorbs an office printer's duplex shift of a millimetre or two; test one sheet against the light first.
- **Footer.** Two lines of Helvetica in grey: the page of the total, the sheet, "Depan" or "Belakang" and the print settings, then the member numbers on that page in the order they read on it (so a backs page lists each row reversed).
- **Cards.** The print order is by member number. Tokens are read with `cards.printCards` when **Buat PDF** is pressed, so a QR replaced after the summary prints current. Each card is drawn by the same code as the saved PNG (`cardSideRenderer` in `card-image.ts`: fonts and art load once, then each member's photo), compressed and written before the next, with a pause between cards so the tab stays responsive. With fixture members, 117 cards took 8 s in Chromium, with no main-thread pause over 50 ms, and made a 21.6 MB file.
- **PDF writer.** `src/lib/pdf.ts` writes PDF 1.7 with no dependency: RGB images compressed losslessly with the browser's `CompressionStream("deflate")` (Flate with the PNG Up predictor), lines, and Helvetica (a standard font, not embedded) for the footer. Each part stays its own Blob chunk, so a large file is never copied into one buffer. `pdf-lib` was not used: its last release was in 2021, and it decodes and re-deflates every PNG in JavaScript, the slow step for hundreds of 0.77-megapixel images.
- **Manifest.** `kartu-anggota-gdgoc-ipb-<date>-manifes.csv` lists each card's member number, name, community tag, the photo actually drawn (Foto unggahan, Foto Google, or Inisial, including an uploaded or Google photo that failed to load), and its page and slot (1 to 9 in reading order) on the fronts and backs pages, with the formula-safe cells of the other exports (`manifestCsv`).

### Profile photos

Members can upload their own photo for the dashboard and the printed card (#146). It revisits decision 3 of #113 (the Google photo, or the pixel initial): the uploaded photo comes first, then the Google photo from sign-in, then the initial (`photoUrl` in `convex/photos.ts`). Decision 5 stands, so a stranger on the scan page still sees no photo.

- **Upload.** `photos.uploadUrl` gives an active member a Convex storage upload URL. `photos.set` is an action, because only an action can read a stored file: it reads the upload and takes its format and pixel size from the file's own header (`imageFacts` in `src/lib/profile-photo.ts`, PNG, JPEG and WebP), then `photos.attach` checks the caller and the limits: JPEG, PNG or WebP, 5 MB at most, at least 400 × 400 px, and a stored content type that matches the bytes, since that is the type the file is served as. A refused upload is deleted and its reason returned. A file another profile or an assignment submission already uses is refused without being touched.
- **Replace and remove.** Attaching a new photo deletes the previous file. `photos.remove` deletes the member's own photo and file, and the Google photo shows again. Staff remove a member's photo with `photos.removeFor`, along the member hierarchy of blocking a card (admins on members, owners also on admins, no one on an owner or on themselves), logged as a `photo` access change.
- **Cleanup.** An upload that is never attached (a closed tab, a refused crop) is removed by the hourly `assignments.cleanupUploads` after a day, which keeps every file a profile or a submission points to (`storedFileInUse`). Account deletion deletes the photo and counts it as `photos`.
- **Where it shows.** `dashboard.viewer` (with `photoUploaded`), `dashboard.members` and `member` rows for staff, `cards.mine`, and `cards.verify` for staff, door helpers and the holder. On screen: the sidebar's account block, the members table and the member's page for staff, the card front on `/dashboard/kartu`, the staff and holder view of the scan page, and the saved print sheet. The door view shows no photo, and neither does the public site header, which reads only the Better Auth session and ships no Convex client (`MemberAvatar` in `src/components/member-avatar.tsx`, `CardPhoto` on the card).
- **The panel.** "Foto profil" on `/dashboard/profil` (`src/components/dashboard/profile-photo.tsx`) says the photo is printed on the card, so a clear face photo is needed, and shows the card front with the current photo and where it comes from. **Pilih foto** (or **Ganti foto**) takes JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 25 MB and refuses an image under 400 × 400 px before cropping. The crop step is a small canvas cropper with no dependency: drag (or the arrow keys, Shift for bigger steps) to position the square, the **Perbesar** slider (or + and -) to zoom, never further than 400 source pixels across, so the print stays sharp (`maxZoom`, `clampCrop` in `src/lib/profile-photo.ts`). Beside it the card front previews the crop at the card's own 200 px photo box (about 17 mm printed). **Simpan foto** draws the crop on white at 800 × 800 and saves it as JPEG at 0.88, which also drops the original's EXIF data, uploads it with progress, and attaches it; a server refusal keeps the crop open with the reason. **Hapus foto** asks first, then goes back to the Google photo. Staff get **Hapus foto profil** in a member's "Kelola" menu, under Status, for an uploaded photo, along the same hierarchy as the server.

### Profile edits

## Papan skor (Bogor Run leaderboard)

Bogor Run is the runner game in the landing-page footer (`public/games/bogor-run/README.md`). Anyone can play. Saving a score needs a verified Google account that is not deactivated; onboarding is not required. The game's pause and game-over panel shows the weekly and all-time top 10 to everyone, guests included, and links here with "Lihat 100 besar".

### The page

`/dashboard/papan-skor` has two tabs, "Minggu ini" (with the week's dates) and "Sepanjang masa". Both boards stay subscribed, so switching is instant; arrow keys, Home, and End move between tabs. The stats row shows your rank for the period (`#n`, "1.000+" past the 1,000 ranks that are counted, or "—" while hidden), your best, and the period's player and run counts.

The table lists up to 100 visible players with rank, short name, score, and when the score was reached (WIB). Tied scores share a rank; among ties, whoever reached the score first is listed first. Your row is highlighted with a "Kamu" badge, and a "Lihat barismu" link jumps to it when it is below the top 10. An empty board links to the game at `/#join`.

### Names and moderation

The public name is `shortName()` of the onboarding full name, or the Google name without a profile: the first name and the last initial ("Aldio Lisafron" → "Aldio L."). A one-word name has nothing to shorten and is shown whole ("Sukarno" stays "Sukarno"); the privacy page says so. A leading initial such as "M." is skipped, invisible characters are dropped, and names are capped at 24 characters. The name is refreshed whenever the player improves a best. No query returns owner ids, emails, or photos.

The ranked table holds visible players only. Owners and admins also get a staff-only **Disembunyikan** section below it (`#board-hidden`): up to 100 hidden players of the period, highest score first. They are listed apart from the top 100, so hidden players never push anyone off it, and a hidden player with a low score is still listed (up to the cap in [Limits](#limits)). Each row says who hid the player, as which role, and when ("Disembunyikan oleh Rahma D. (pemilik) pada …"), or that the account is deactivated, with a "Nonaktif" badge. "Sembunyikan" and "Tampilkan lagi" ask for confirmation, then call `bogorRun.setHidden`, which hides or restores the player on every board, including bests they set later. Hidden scores stay stored; public ranks are counted without them. The player sees a notice on this page and "disembunyikan admin" in the game.

Who may hide or restore whom is modelled on `dashboard.setActive`:

- Nobody can hide or restore themselves.
- Admins act only on members. Only owners act on admins and on other owners.
- An admin cannot restore a player an owner hid. Hiding a player who is already hidden changes nothing, so the original hider (and their role) stays on record.
- A player hidden only because their account is deactivated comes back when it is reactivated, and `setHidden` refuses to restore them (`INACTIVE`). Restoring a deactivated player whom staff also hid clears the staff hide; the score returns on reactivation, and the confirmation says so.

`board` applies the same rules per row (`canHide`, `canRestore`), and the page offers a button only where the server would accept it. Otherwise the row shows a lock with "Khusus pemilik" or "Tampil saat akun aktif", and your own row shows nothing. A refusal from the server (`FORBIDDEN` or `INACTIVE`, with an Indonesian message) appears in the confirmation row.

`gamePlayers` keeps the audit: `hiddenAt` while staff hide the player, `hiddenBy` and `hiddenByRole` for the last hide (kept after a restore), and `restoredAt` and `restoredBy` for the last restore. `gameBests.hidden` is derived from it and from the account: a best is hidden while staff hide the player or while the account is deactivated. `syncBoardVisibility` recomputes it, and both `setHidden` and `dashboard.setActive` call it.

### Functions

| Function | Access | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bogorRun.issueRun` | Public mutation | A random seed and its signed token. Writes nothing. |
| `bogorRun.submitRun` | Verified, active account | Replays a crashed or finished run and saves its score |
| `bogorRun.leaderboard` | Public query | Top 10 visible players and the viewer's own standing |
| `bogorRun.board` | Members (`requireMember`) | Top 100 visible players with `achievedAt`, `canHide`, and player/run counts; staff also get `hiddenEntries` (up to 100, with the audit fields, `inactive`, and `canRestore`) and `canModerate` |
| `bogorRun.setHidden` | Staff (`requireStaff`), under the rules above | Hides or restores a player on every board |
| `bogorRun.pruneRuns` | Internal, daily cron | Deletes `gameRuns` rows older than 30 days |

`submitRun` returns `{ ok: false, code, message }` for expected rejections (`UNAUTHENTICATED`, `DEACTIVATED`, `INVALID`, `TOO_EARLY`, `EXPIRED`, `USED`, `OUTDATED`) with an Indonesian message, instead of throwing. A rank is `null` when the player is hidden or past 1,000. `setHidden` throws a `ConvexError` with `{ code, message }`: `FORBIDDEN`, `INACTIVE`, or `NOT_FOUND`.

### Replay verification

1. **Token.** The browser sends its `ENGINE_VERSION` (`engine.ts`). `issueRun` rolls a uint32 seed and signs `{ v: 2, engine, seed, issuedAt, nonce }` (base64url JSON plus an HMAC-SHA256 signature, `convex/bogorRunToken.ts`). A browser on another engine version gets `{ outdated: true }`; it stops asking for tickets and says to reload the page for ranked play. Clients from before engine versions send none and count as engine 1; once the engine moves on they get `null` and play unranked. Tokens signed before engine versions (`v: 1`) still verify as engine 1. The key is `HMAC(BETTER_AUTH_SECRET, "gdgoc:bogor-run:token:v1")`, so no new secret is needed; without `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` it returns `null` and the game plays unranked. The browser keeps one ticket ready while the game is on the page (`keepTicketFresh` in `online.ts`). A ticket is used only within 10 minutes of its fetch, and never after the week it was issued in has ended. It is renewed shortly before its 10 minutes are up, right after its week ends, and as soon as a hidden tab comes back. Hidden tabs fetch nothing, and without a ticket the page retries every minute. A run that starts while a ticket is still on its way holds its first tick for up to 1 s, then adopts the ticket and keeps the keys pressed meanwhile. If none arrives, the run plays unranked.
2. **Play.** The browser and Convex run the same engine (`src/lib/bogor-run/engine.ts`): fixed 1/120 s ticks, a seeded PRNG stored on the run, world-space spawning, and only arithmetic that gives identical results everywhere. The browser records each jump, duck, and stand that changes the state as `[tick, code]`.
3. **Submit.** When a signed-in player crashes or reaches the one-hour finish line, the run is submitted automatically: the token, the inputs, the end tick, and the score. The inputs always travel as a packed base-36 string (`packInputs`), because Convex rejects argument arrays longer than 8,192 elements.
4. **Checks,** in order: a verified, active account; a valid signature and exact claim shape; a nonce no other account has used (`USED`; a resubmit from the same account gets its current standing back); a token from this engine version (`OUTDATED`); an end tick from 1 to 432,000 and a non-negative integer score; at least 97% of the simulated time, minus 1 s, since `issuedAt` (`TOO_EARLY`); at most the simulated time plus 12 hours (`EXPIRED`). Finally the replay must be valid, end on exactly the end tick (a crash, or the finish line at 432,000), and produce exactly the claimed score. A rejected attempt does not use up its nonce, so an honest retry still works.
5. **Save.** An accepted run adds a `gameRuns` row without its inputs, updates the week's and all-time `gameBests` only when the score improves, and increments the `gameStats` counters.

A guest who chooses "Masuk untuk simpan skor" has the run kept in `sessionStorage` (`gdgoc:bogor-run:pending:v1`) during the Google sign-in, which returns to `/?skor=simpan#join` (or `/?skor=gagal#join` on failure). The landing removes the parameter, scrolls to the game, replays the run locally to show its end, and submits it. The pending run is read once, and ignored 30 minutes after game over. A guest who comes back without signing in, for example with Back from Google, sees the run again with "Skor tadi belum tersimpan. Masuk untuk menyimpannya." and the sign-in button. A signed-in player whose session cannot get its Convex token sees a connection error with "Coba simpan lagi", not the sign-in button.

### The one-hour finish line

A run that survives `LIMITS.maxTicks` (432,000 ticks, one hour) ends there as finished, not crashed. The engine sets the phase to `"over"` with `finished: true` and no hit, and `replayRun` returns `finished: true`. `submitRun` accepts it like a crash, under the same pace check, so it saves no earlier than about 58 minutes after its ticket was issued. The game shows "Selesai! 1 jam penuh" with the Dino standing. The score depends only on how long the run lasted, so every finisher scores 137,403; among equal scores, whoever saved first is listed first. After five minutes the course keeps getting harder (see the game's README), so reaching the hour takes a strong player.

### Run records

The daily `remove Bogor Run run records after 30 days` cron (`convex/crons.ts`, 20:41 UTC, which is 03:41 WIB) runs `bogorRun.pruneRuns`. It deletes `gameRuns` rows saved more than 30 days earlier (`RUN_RETENTION`), 1,000 at a time, and reschedules itself while more remain. Single use still holds: a token is `EXPIRED` at most 13 hours after it was issued (the simulated hour plus 12 hours), long before its row is deleted. Bests, player records, and the `gameStats` counters are kept.

### Weeks

A week starts on Monday at 00:00 WIB (UTC+7) and is keyed by that Monday's date (`weekKey`). A run counts toward the week its ticket was issued in, not the week it was saved. The browser drops a ticket when its week ends, so an honest run counts for the week it started in: one started late on Sunday stays in that week even if it ends on Monday, and the game then says "Tersimpan · peringkat #n minggu lalu". Clients pass the current week explicitly (the dashboard page from a ticking clock), because Convex query results do not re-run as time passes.

The server accepts a run until its simulated time plus 12 hours after the ticket was issued. So last week's board can still change until about 13:00 WIB on Monday, from a ticket issued just before midnight and held for a full hour's run. This is by design.

### Limits

- Replay verification cannot tell a bot from a person, and a bot needs no real-time play. The pace check only proves the ticket is old enough: it was issued at least 97% of the simulated time, minus 1 s, before the save. A script can fetch tickets without signing in, several at once, compute a run in a fraction of a second, and save it once each ticket has aged. The demo autopilot ships in the browser bundle and plays to the finish line, so such a run scores the ceiling of 137,403, the same as any finisher. This risk is accepted: staff hide suspicious players with the moderation above.
- Pauses are not recorded, so pausing to think cannot be detected; only the minimum real duration is enforced.
- There is no per-account rate limit, and `issueRun` needs no sign-in. Each bogus submission costs at most one hour of replay, well under the 1 s mutation limit (`docs/VERIFICATION.md` has the measurements). Every accepted run counts in "Permainan tercatat", so one account can inflate that counter with many short runs; a run with no input is accepted about 2 s after its ticket was issued. Add a rate limit, with a new error code, if abuse appears.
- A run with more than 10,000 inputs plays unranked, and the game says why.
- Every play view shows at least 320 world units ahead of the dino (`MIN_AHEAD` in `view.ts`), so phones warn as early as laptops. A 0.3 s reaction plus half a jump at top speed needs about 283. Phones zoom out below the old 1.3 minimum scale to reach it, and the dino sits at 8% of the width: it is 43 px wide at 390 px and 36 px at 320 px. Before this (#73) a 390 px phone showed 214 units and a 320 px phone 168, and the gameplay review's bot lasted a median of 7.6 minutes at 390 px against about 22 at 1440 px. The engine is unchanged, so scores stay comparable.
- The staff list shows the 100 highest hidden scores of a period. With more hidden players than that, restore a lower one from a week in which they rank higher, or in the Convex dashboard.
- The server replays only the current engine. Any change to physics or generation must bump `ENGINE_VERSION`; then tabs still on the old client get `OUTDATED` and a reload prompt rather than `INVALID`, and runs they finish in the meantime are not ranked. A whole-hour fingerprint test in `engine.test.ts` fails until the version is bumped and the new fingerprint recorded. A second test pins the first five minutes to the engine from before the late-game ramp.
- Ranks are counted exactly up to 1,000, which keeps rank reads bounded; past that the rank is `null`.

## Data model

- `memberProfiles`: adds optional `role`, `deactivatedAt`, `accessUpdatedBy`, `memberType` (`member`/`core`/`bod`), `division`, `techSupportHandler`, `photoStorageId` with `photoUploadedAt` for an uploaded photo, and `instagramChangedAt` (#235), a `by_completed` index, a `by_photo` index, and a `search_name` full-text index on `fullName`.
- `assignments`: optional `slug`, `maxScore`, `rubric` (name and max per criterion), `reviewers` (account IDs), `audience` (community tags; missing means Core Team), and `cohortId` with `cohortRoles` for cohort assignments, plus `by_status_due`, `by_updated` and `by_cohort` indexes.
- `assignmentSlugs`: every slug an assignment has used (`by_slug`, `by_assignment`).
- `assignmentSubmissions`: one row per member per assignment (`by_assignment_owner`), with the latest review in optional `score`, `breakdown`, `feedback`, `reviewedAt`, `reviewedBy`, and `revisionRequestedAt` while a revision is requested.
- `submissionReviews`: one row per review action, with `score`, `breakdown`, and `revisionRequested` for revision requests (`by_submission`).
- `submissionFiles`: pending or attached uploads (`by_storage`, `by_submission`, `by_owner_assignment`, `by_assignment`).
- `gameRuns`: one row per accepted Bogor Run run, with seed, nonce, times, end tick, score, and week, but not its inputs (`by_nonce`, `by_owner`, `by_submitted`). Rows are deleted 30 days after they were saved.
- `gameBests`: each player's best per period, `"all"` or a week key, with the public short name and a `hidden` flag, set while staff hide the player or the account is deactivated (`by_owner_period`, `by_period_hidden_score`).
- `gamePlayers`: staff moderation per player, which also applies to later bests: `hiddenAt` while hidden, `hiddenBy` and `hiddenByRole` of the last hide, and `restoredAt` and `restoredBy` of the last restore (`by_owner`).
- `gameStats`: player and run counters per period, so the board never scans every run (`by_period`).
- `accessLog`: one row per access change: `at`, `actorId`, `targetId`, `change` (`role`, `reviewer`, `active`, `memberType`, `assignmentReviewer`, `deleted`, `tagConfirmed`, `tagSelfChanged`, `enrolmentRole`, `enrolmentStatus`, `enrolmentEmail`, `card`, `cardIssued`, `gatheringHelper`, `techSupportHandler`, `photo`, `cohortMentor`), `from` and `to`, `assignmentId` for assignment reviewers, `cohortId` and `enrolmentId` for enrolment changes (`cohortId` also for mentors), and `gatheringId` for door helpers (`by_at`, `by_target`). Account IDs only; an enrolment change for someone who has not signed in has an empty `targetId`.
- `cohorts`: `slug`, `name`, `roles` (`key`, `name`), `status`, optional `attendanceTarget`, and who created it (`by_slug`).
- `enrolments`: `cohortId`, `email`, `name`, `role`, `status` (`active`/`withdrawn`), who imported it and when, optional `ownerId` and `claimedAt` once claimed, who withdrew it, and an optional `teamId` (`by_cohort_email`, `by_email`, `by_owner`, `by_cohort_role`, `by_team`).
- `cohortMentors`: `cohortId`, `ownerId`, `role`, and who added it (`by_owner_cohort`, `by_cohort_role`).
- `leagueTeams`: `season`, `name`, `track` (`software`/`uiux`/`business`), `code`, `inviteToken` (the invite code), optional `captainId` (absent only on a flagged team), optional `submittedAt` (#180), optional `registration` (`paid`, `importedAt`, `importedBy`), and who created it (`by_code`, `by_invite`, `by_season`).
- `leagueTeamMembers`: `teamId`, `season`, `ownerId`, `joinedAt` (`by_team`, `by_owner`).
- `teams`: `cohortId`, `name`, who created it and when (`by_cohort`); members are the enrolments with its `teamId`.
- `memberCards`: one per account: `ownerId`, `period` (the chapter year, e.g. `2026/27`), `number`, `token`, `issuedAt`, optional `reissuedAt`, and `revokedAt` with `revokedBy` while staff block it (`by_owner`, `by_token`, `by_number`).
- `cardCounters`: how many member numbers each chapter year has given out (`by_period`).
- `gatherings`: `title`, `startsAt`, `endsAt`, `venue`, `audience` (community tags), `status` (`draft`/`open`/`closed`), `helpers` (account IDs), and who created it (`by_starts`, `by_status`).
- `checkIns`: one per member and gathering: `gatheringId`, `ownerId`, `checkedInAt`, `checkedInBy`, `method` (`scan`/`manual`), and `outsideAudience` (`by_gathering_owner`, `by_owner`).
- `techSupportRequests`: `ownerId`, `competition`, `competitionUrl`, `deadline` (YYYY-MM-DD in WIB), `participation` (`team`/`solo`), optional `teamName`, `helpKinds`, `progress`, `status` (`new`/`matched`/`inProgress`/`closed`), optional `mentorName` and `note`, `submittedAt`, `updatedAt`, and optional `matchedAt` (the first match, never cleared), `closedAt` and `handledBy` (`by_owner`, `by_submitted`).
- `accountDeletions`: one row per deleted account with `deletedAt`, `deletedBy` and how many rows of each kind went, without the member's name or email (`by_deleted`). `enrolments`, `mentorGrants`, `cards`, `checkIns`, `helperGrants`, `techSupportRequests`, `photos` and `leagueTeams` are optional, since earlier rows lack them.

All schema changes are additive, so the previous frontend keeps working during a backend-first deploy.
